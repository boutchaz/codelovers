We started AgroGina because we saw a gap: software for large agricultural operations that understands the realities Moroccan farmers face, with a path toward international standards and useful AI integrations.

Managing a farm means connecting what happens on the ground with the people, resources and decisions behind it. We want to build around that connection, with the people who know it best.

## Why start here?

Local context shapes how agricultural work gets done. The way teams record work, share information, manage inputs and move between the field and the office matters as much as the features on a product page.

We believe there is room for a locally grounded solution with broader ambition: one that helps agricultural operations structure their work and build reliable records, while remaining useful in their everyday conditions.

That is our reason for building AgroGina. It is also a direction we need to keep testing with farms, rather than treating our assumptions as settled answers.

## What AgroGina is

We’re building a farm management platform that connects agricultural operations with the business behind them.

Its scope brings together farms and parcels, field tasks, workers, inputs, stock, purchasing, sales, costs and accounting. These activities are connected in practice. A field task involves people, uses resources and creates costs; the records should help teams follow those connections.

Our aim is to help teams understand what happened, what it required and what needs attention next, without repeatedly reconstructing the story across separate tools.

We also want to connect those operational records with observations from the field, IoT measurements and satellite analysis. Each source can contribute something different.

## Trust starts with evidence people understand

Farms do not all build confidence in the same way.

For some teams, trust starts with something concrete: a field visit, a soil sample, a meter reading or a sensor installed on their own land. A satellite map alone will not answer their questions.

Other teams already work extensively with data. They want to know where a measurement came from, when it was collected, whether it is complete and how it was processed. They need integrity and traceability as well as a useful visualization.

AgroGina needs to respect both approaches.

Ground observations provide essential context for checking remote estimates. IoT can add measurements between field visits. Satellite imagery can help teams examine patterns across larger areas.

These sources do not measure the same thing, and none is automatically correct in every situation. A sensor may need calibration. A field observation may lack a date or location. A satellite observation may be outdated or unsuitable for the question being asked.

We want to make the origin, age and limitations of information understandable, including when sources disagree or data is missing. Showing uncertainty is part of building trust.

## What AgroGina is not

AgroGina does not replace a farmer’s experience, an agronomist’s judgment or the people responsible for operational and financial decisions.

We are not presenting satellite analysis as certainty, sensors as infallible or AI as an automatic answer. We are not claiming a complete integration with every device or a finished solution to every agricultural problem.

We also do not claim that software alone guarantees compliance with international standards. The relevant standards, evidence requirements and validation processes need to be identified with the operations and specialists concerned.

Our responsibility is to build useful tools and make the evidence behind them clear.

## Where we are today

AgroGina already has farm clients, and we are validating the platform with farms. Alongside that work, we continue developing and improving the core management workflows: farms and parcels, tasks and workforce, inventory, purchasing and sales, and accounting. Satellite analysis is also part of the work.

Our current focus is connecting these workflows and checking how they behave as a system. There is still work to do to resolve issues, verify complete journeys and improve the experience.

IoT and structured ground observations belong in our direction. The appropriate devices, integrations and validation workflows still need to be defined and tested with farms.

The platform is already being used and validated with farm clients while development continues. That does not mean every capability or future integration described here is available or field-validated today.

## What we need next

Validation with farms is already under way. Our next step is to deepen that work and use client feedback to improve connected workflows against the practical needs of agricultural operations.

We want to understand:

- Where information gets lost between the field and the office.
- Which decisions depend on field observations, measurements or historical records.
- Which equipment and sensors teams already use, and how they access that data.
- What makes a measurement trustworthy enough to act on.
- Which reporting, traceability and standards requirements matter to each operation.
- How connectivity, language and working habits affect the tools people can use.

Carefully scoped validation will help us choose what to improve first, with clear expectations about data access, responsibilities and what each test should establish.

## Where we are going

Our first priority is a dependable operational foundation: connected workflows and records that teams can understand and check.

From there, we want to bring field observations, suitable IoT measurements and satellite analysis into a coherent view, with clear provenance, quality checks and traceable changes.

That foundation can support better reporting and the records required by relevant international standards. It can also make AI integrations more useful: helping teams interpret information while making their inputs, limitations and need for human judgment clear.

These are priorities to validate, rather than a promise of fixed delivery dates. We will share our progress, limitations and lessons as the product develops.
